# Generation lenses

Each Stage 2 generator works through exactly one lens. Different lenses approach the scope from different angles, so the seed pool covers more ground and duplicates less. When several lenses land on the same job, that is evidence the job is real, and clustering uses it. A generator may produce a seed that also fits another lens; that's fine.

All lenses share two filters:
- The seed must exploit something current AI can do that existing solutions don't. Use `landscape.md`, not your memory, for what current AI can do.
- Prefer seeds that could run self-serve, with no human work per user, and whose users can be reached without a sales call. Don't discard a seed that fails this if its job is strong; note its `ops_profile` honestly, because clustering may find a low-ops shape for the same job.

## workflow — Recurring work
Map the scope's recurring work end to end: inputs, handoffs, checks and outputs. Find the steps people repeat every day or week that are slow, tedious or error-prone.
- Look for steps where unstructured information (documents, email, chat, calls, images) is turned into decisions or structured data by people.
- Look for reformatting, reconciling, checking, chasing and reporting work, and for work people pay freelancers or agencies to do.
- Strong signals: the step recurs weekly or more, the person doing it can judge the output themselves, and people complain about it in public.

## capability — New capability unlock
Start from what AI can now do that it couldn't 12–18 months ago, according to `landscape.md`: for example agents and computer use, long context, vision, real-time voice, reasoning or much cheaper inference. Ask where in the scope each one unlocks something that was impractical before.
- Prefer capabilities that crossed a usefulness threshold recently. Opportunities usually appear there before the incumbents move.
- For each seed, name the specific capability and the evidence that it now works.

## gap — Incumbent gap
Find where users are unhappy with existing tools and services in the scope.
- Search review sites, forums, community threads, social posts and complaints about incumbent products, including "<incumbent> alternative" queries.
- Look for jobs the incumbents leave to spreadsheets, copy-paste or manual work, and for users the incumbents serve badly because they are too small or span several platforms.
- Also check what incumbents have recently shipped in AI. A gap the incumbent closed last month isn't a gap.

## founder — Founder insight
Generate seeds from the user background in `scope.md`: jobs this person has done or watched others do, communities they belong to, and tools they wished existed.
- Use their role and industry to infer the recurring pains, the people they can reach directly and the vocabulary those people use.
- If no background was given, use the most common practitioner roles in the scope instead, and note that this lens ran without user context.

## surface — Distribution surface
Start from where the scope's users already discover and install small tools: marketplaces and app stores for the platforms they use, browser extension stores, AI assistant plugin and agent directories, open-source ecosystems, and the communities and search terms they use. Seeds from this lens come with a channel built in, which is what fast signal needs.
- Look for listings that are popular but badly reviewed, repeated feature requests, "is there a tool that…" threads, and categories with few or stale listings.
- Check each surface's listing rules and review times, because they decide how fast a probe can go live.
